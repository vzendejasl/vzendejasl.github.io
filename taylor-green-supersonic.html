Supersonic Taylor–Green vortex

This is the same flow as the low Mach number Taylor–Green vortex, a periodic box of deterministic vortices that breaks down into turbulence, but it starts at a supersonic Mach number. Compressibility matters from the start, and sharp fronts form in the fields while the vortices break down, so the method has to capture shocks as well as resolve turbulence.

These are high-order finite element (FEM) calculations, so the mesh is counted in elements. In Q2Q1 the velocity and the mesh are continuous quadratic (Q2) elements, which gives about two kinematic nodes per element in each direction, and the internal energy is discontinuous linear (Q1). Kinematic DOFs are the degrees of freedom of the velocity, counting every component.

Code
MARBL, an arbitrary Lagrangian–Eulerian (ALE) code, based on the high-order finite element methods of Dobrev et al. (2012), Anderson et al. (2018) and Anderson et al. (2015), with more detail in the MAPP report
Viscosity
Artificial viscosity for shock capturing and for the dissipation in the Taylor–Green runs: Bello-Maldonado et al. (2020)
