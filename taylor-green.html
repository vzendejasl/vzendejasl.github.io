Taylor–Green vortex

The Taylor–Green vortex is a periodic box of fluid that starts as a smooth, regular pattern of vortices and ends up turbulent. The initial conditions are fully deterministic, with no noise and no random forcing, so everything that happens afterwards comes from the flow itself and from the numerical method.

The flow starts out laminar. Shearing between the vortices tilts and stretches the vortex tubes, which is the stretching term in the vorticity equation on the Richtmyer–Meshkov page. Stretching squeezes vorticity into thinner and thinner structures, the large vortices break down into small ones, and the flow makes the transition from laminar to turbulent.

These are high-order finite element (FEM) calculations, so the mesh is counted in elements. In Q2Q1 the velocity and the mesh are continuous quadratic (Q2) elements, which gives about two kinematic nodes per element in each direction, and the internal energy is discontinuous linear (Q1). Kinematic DOFs are the degrees of freedom of the velocity, counting every component.

Code
MARBL, an arbitrary Lagrangian–Eulerian (ALE) code, based on the high-order finite element methods of Dobrev et al. (2012), Anderson et al. (2018) and Anderson et al. (2015), with more detail in the MAPP report
